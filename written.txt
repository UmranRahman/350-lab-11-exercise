2a. The batched version converged to about 9 FPS, 
while the unbatched sf::Sprite version (main_nobatch.cpp) 
converged to about also close to 9 FPS. The two are close because I ran 
under WSL, where OpenGL appears to use a software renderer, 
so rasterizing and additively blending 10,000 overlapping 
sprites dominates the frame time. Batching still replaces 
roughly 10,000 draw calls with one and, in part 2, moves 
the per-vertex transforms onto the GPU, but that saving is 
small compared to the rasterization cost in this setup.

2b. One method is to enable the depth test 
(glEnable(GL_DEPTH_TEST), and we already 
request a 24-bit depth buffer) and have the 
vertex shader output a per-sprite depth as 
the z component of gl_Position, for example 
from a layer value stored per sprite. The 
rasterizer then compares depths per fragment 
and keeps the nearest one, so sprites are 
layered correctly without sorting them on the CPU. 
The caveat is that fully transparent pixels still 
write depth and can hide sprites behind them, so we 
would discard transparent fragments in the fragment 
shader or sort and blend back to front.
